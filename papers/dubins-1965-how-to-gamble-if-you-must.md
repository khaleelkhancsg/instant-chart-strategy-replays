## What it says

Dubins and Savage study a gambler who must reach a target fortune and can
choose the stakes. In a **subfair** game (negative edge), **bold play** --
staking the smaller of the whole fortune and the amount needed to reach the
goal -- maximises the probability of reaching it: fewer bets means less time
for the negative drift to work. In a favourable game the reverse holds: small,
**timid** bets let the positive drift accumulate while the noise averages out.
Later work found exceptions with house limits or discounting.

## How to use it here

It answers the earlier question in this project about win rate, leverage and
the evaluation. A book with no edge after costs is a subfair game: the fewest,
largest bets that can reach the target maximise the pass rate, but cannot raise
it above about room / (room + target) (note-combine-first-passage). A book with
a real edge wants the opposite: enough trades, sized small enough that noise
cannot reach the floor first -- limited only by the 30-day deadline
(browne-1999).
